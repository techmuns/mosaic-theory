# The ai-value-migration note is superseded — "the de-rating was indiscriminate" was wrong

**WHAT.** The operator replaced the `ai-value-migration` analyst notes on 2026-09-01, hours after
writing them, and asked for the correction to be LOGGED RATHER THAN OVERWRITTEN. The superseded
claim is reproduced verbatim below so the reversal is inspectable; the live note carries the
correction in the operator's own words.

**THE CLAIM THAT DID NOT SURVIVE.** The first note argued the market had repriced the driver
"violently and asymmetrically" and that the moated names were "drowned alongside the casualties" —
i.e. the de-rating was indiscriminate, and the mispricing was the good names being sold with the
bad. The operator's correction:

> the selloff overwhelmingly hit horizontal, general-purpose SaaS while vertical software with
> domain expertise, regulatory moats and embedded workflows was structurally insulated, so that
> claim was wrong

**WHY IT MATTERS BEYOND THE WORDING.** If the market already separates regulatory depth,
proprietary workflow data and hard-to-rip-out systems of record from ordinary software, then it is
applying close to the same discriminator the 357 verdicts were adjudicated on. That reframes the
168 ratifies as a QUALITY SCREEN rather than a mispricing — durability already paid for is not a
trade — and moves the tradeable question to where these verdicts DISAGREE with the line the market
has drawn. The sheet cannot answer that: it carries no multiples.

**A HARDER CONSTRAINT SURFACED WITH IT.** The new note names a measurement problem prior to either
kill criterion: with 354 model-agnostic names against three frontier-lab proxies, and GOOGL and TSM
absent from the scan, there is no "from" leg. A migration needs both sides to be measurable, so
**the thesis as written cannot be falsified on this universe** — which is a defect in the
instrument, not in the theme, and it stands whatever the market has or has not repriced.

**ONE CLAIM CHECKED AGAINST THE DATA.** The note says `machine_grade` "was never refreshed when the
65 REVISIT rows were corrected". Literally true — it changed on 0 of 65 — but it could not have:
`machine_grade` is the ROW's `exposure_basis` and does not depend on which fact is displayed, so the
correction moved `grade_driver_tie` (37 rows) and left the grade alone. The substance is right by a
different mechanism: 353 of 357 rows sit at `committed-capacity-or-capex` because the scan reads
10-K text, so DGXX's $1.1B Cerebras note and AVGO's $10.8B AI line never lift the grade. The note is
stored as written; the operator was told the mechanism and can amend it.

**THE SUPERSEDED NOTE, VERBATIM (4 items):**

1. The market isn't making the mistake the thesis names — it's already repriced the driver, violently and asymmetrically: seat software below the S&P's multiple for the first time (~22.7x from 84x) while AI infra sits at highs, yet AVGO fell 13% on a +143% AI quarter. Abundant capability is priced as annihilation above the model and infinite demand below it; both can't be true, and the only leg plausibly cheap is the moated ABOVE names drowned alongside the casualties.

2. The evidence mostly isn't in the filings — only 4 of 357 names carry binding contracts with terms, and the modal ratified 10-K says “no AI monetization broken out.” The proof lives in earnings releases and contracts (AVGO's $10.8B AI line, ARM's doubling data-center royalties, CRWV's $60.7B RPO) and in the tape.

3. The losers are anyone selling a unit abundance shrinks: seats (TEAM, WDAY, MNDY), billable hours (EPAM, CNXC — KFRC named GenAI in its own filing), and substitutable data middlemen, with ZoomInfo the cleanest corpse.

4. I'd drop it if the catch-up window widens past ~6 months (it already moved 3→4), if near-frontier open weights keep pricing at frontier levels — capability converging without price commoditizing kills the supply-side argument — or if the moated ABOVE names never decouple from the seat casualties, leaving the migration no destination that re-rates.

**WHAT IT COSTS / WHAT IS ACCEPTED.**

1. **`analyst_notes` HAS NO SUPERSESSION STRUCTURE.** It is a flat list of strings, so a replaced
   note leaves no trace in the artifact a reader sees — only this record. A second reversal on the
   same theme would be invisible in the same way. FALSIFIER: if a note is superseded again, the
   schema owes a dated/superseded shape rather than another record pointing sideways.
2. **The thesis card still asserts the market "still prices raw frontier-model IP as the centre of
   AI value capture."** The new note says the market has already repriced it. Both are the
   operator's and both are live; superseding a declared card is their call and is not taken here.
