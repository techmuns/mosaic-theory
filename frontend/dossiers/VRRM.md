### Bull case — the decline is overdone
- Government Solutions — the primary value engine at 47% of revenue — grew service revenue 4% in Q1 2026 driven by 12% growth outside New York City, showing the core motor still turning. [11]
- Trailing-12-month new Government bookings of ~$71M and a fresh Los Angeles award point to a long-term recurring-contract pipeline feeding future revenue. [11]
- Absolute EBITDA has risen every year through the de-rating window ($229M FY21 → $362M FY25, $358M LTM), so the falling margin % is a mix/reclass optical, not eroding earnings power. [12]
- The company has delivered on its EBITDA guide floor 8/10 resolved and met FY2025, with the fresh FY26 revised guide dated 2026-05-26 already reflecting the Avis loss — a credible checkpoint rather than an unhedged number. [23]

### Bear case — the decline is real

- Avis Budget — a >10% of total revenue customer — issued a termination notice effective September 2026, a real structural revenue loss, not an optical one. [14]
- Management guided FY26 adjusted EBITDA down to $380–385M from the prior $405–415M and FY27 consensus EBITDA falls to $333M — a real forward step-down, and the low forward TEV/EBITDA ~4.3x reflects a shrinking, not merely cheap, earnings base. [27]

- Short interest is rising fast (+42.8% to 11.1M sh, 7.3% of float) into the decline, signalling positioning against a recovery. [30]

### Decisive question
**Does the durable Government Solutions recurring pipeline replace the lost Avis revenue and stabilise forward EBITDA, or does the Avis termination plus NYC pricing pressure mark a step-down to a permanently lower earnings base?**

### …evidence for
- Government Solutions grew service revenue 4% in Q1 2026 driven by 12% growth outside NYC, with ~$71M trailing-12-month new bookings feeding a recurring pipeline. [11]
- A newly awarded Los Angeles speed-safety program — the largest in California — adds to the long-term contract backlog after the Avis notice. [31]
- Absolute EBITDA rose to $362M FY25 and the business remains FCF-positive and self-funding, giving runway to redeploy resources off the lost customer. [12]

### …evidence against
- Avis termination removes $135–145M of 2026-annualized revenue and $120–125M of segment profit — a hole larger than the ~$71M annual Government bookings run-rate. [13]
- The NYC contract pricing change more than offset new-camera installation growth, so the largest Government account is not accretive at the margin. [11]
- FY27 consensus EBITDA of $333M sits below FY26 $382M, implying consensus sees the base stepping down, not recovering. [32]

### Key unknowns
- Whether the >10%-of-revenue Commercial Services relationship (Avis) is fully replaceable and the pace of cost re-allocation — management states resources are being reallocated but gives no quantified offset.
- Covenant terms are UNKNOWN (extraction failed) — a missing expected disclosure; without covenant headroom the survival read on the 2028 Term Loan wall is incomplete, a bearish opacity risk.
- Whether the current CEO vacancy resolves with a permanent appointment and continuity of the Government Solutions/MOSAIC strategy.
- Whether the FY26 revised guide ($985–995M revenue, $380–385M EBITDA) already fully captures the Avis loss or further Commercial Services churn follows — the guide is fresh but the print date 2026-08-05 is the checkpoint.
- The justified-vs-residual split of the peer discount cannot be resolved from a HETEROGENEOUS peer set spanning $430M–$15.9B; the human must judge comparability before reading any peer multiple.
