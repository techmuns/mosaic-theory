The thesis

FORECAST US caffeinated beverage spending has grown 6% annually since 2019 (energy drinks 8%), with net 19% of surveyed consumers intending to increase energy drink consumption, up from ~5% in 2019/2021, and Morgan Stanley expects the pace to continue through 2030. ⟨cited figures: forecast⟩ C-fbcd50ef702e9b C-446a477167fa1d C-1e6e7e5da00ed1 C-8059ff28448912 C-af147254c41019
Details

What changed

  • 61% of consumers increasing energy drink consumption cited needing more energy. (2026)
  • Energy drinks are creating new consumption occasions rather than simply replacing coffee or soft drinks.
Details

Effect on profits, margins or demand

  • Industry shiftConsumers add new caffeine occasions (energy, flavor, convenience) rather than substituting coffee, expanding the overall market.
  • VolumesEnergy drink consumption frequency and penetration rise; net purchase intent at 19% vs ~5% prior surveys.
  • PricingMakers use targeted pricing to raise prices gradually while keeping energy drinks relatively affordable vs soft drinks and coffee.
Details

Strongest evidence

  • 19% — net purchase intent, energy drinks (next three months, actual; Morgan Stanley, 2026-09-02)
  • 8% — energy drinks spending growth (since 2019, actual; Morgan Stanley, 2026-09-02)
  • 42 drinks per month — energy drinks per month, GLP-1 users (2026, actual; Morgan Stanley, 2026-09-02)
Details

Why Watch

  • Catalyst — no verified dislocation, dated catalyst or near-term change in a fundamental.
Details

What could invalidate it

  • Decline in net purchase intent in subsequent AlphaWise surveys.
  • Evidence of energy drinks substituting rather than adding occasions.
Details

Next check
