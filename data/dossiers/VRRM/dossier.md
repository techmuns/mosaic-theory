## Synthesis

**Evidence balance:** mixed — a genuine permanent customer-loss step-down and cut forward estimates offset by a still-FCF-positive, high-margin, self-funding base at trough multiples.

### Bull case (overshoot / mean-revert)
- Absolute EBITDA has RISEN through the period ($229M FY21 → $362M FY25 → $358M LTM) even as the margin % drifted from 42% to 37% — the % erosion is the optical read, not shrinking cash generation. [8]
- The business remains FCF-positive ($53.9M LTM levered FCF) and self-funding, with interest coverage of 5.6x and no near-term maturity wall (earliest 2028 term loan, 2029 notes). [9]
- Management issued fresh, cross-source-VERIFIED FY26 guidance already absorbing the Avis termination — revenue $985–995M, adj-EBITDA $380–385M, adj-EPS $1.19–1.25, FCF $140–150M — implying forward adj P/E ~3.5x and fwd EV/adj-EBITDA ~4.3x. [10]
- The company is buying back stock (164.7M → 151.9M shares, -7.7% over ~1.7y), an owner-friendly use of its self-funding FCF rather than a distressed cash posture. [41]
- The guidance-delivery track record is intact post-shock: adj-EPS actual≥floor 3/3, revenue 8/10, EBITDA 8/10, with the most recent FY2025 all MET. [50]
- The name's own EBITDA margin (37%) is roughly double the peer-median 18% and its beta lower (0.4 vs 0.8), so part of the discount is un-earned on a margin/quality basis — posing whether the gap is RESIDUAL overshoot rather than earned; but the peer set is HETEROGENEOUS ($430M–$15.9B caps) so this is a confidence-gated input, not a clean benchmark. [55]
- One covering broker (CJS) UPGRADED to Outperform with a $12 target on 2026-06-09 after the shock, against a current ~$4.23 price. [56]

### Bear case (terminal / value-trap)
- Leverage is elevated versus peers and the company carries a sub-investment-grade profile — net-debt/EBITDA 2.9x, total-debt/EBITDA 3.0x vs peer-median 1.9x, S&P issuer rating BB- with weakest-tier Solvency score — raising the stakes if the forward EBITDA base steps down further. [11]
- The drawdown is driven by a discrete, structural customer loss — Avis Budget (>10% of total revenue) served a termination notice — which management itself quantifies as a $135–145M annualized revenue hit and $120–125M segment-profit hit; this is a real permanent-impairment signal, not an optical timing artifact. [12]
- Forward estimates have been cut sharply and broadly — FY26 EBITDA consensus -16%, EBIT -26%, GAAP net income -29% (12m→latest) — and revision breadth is one-sided (Revenue FY26 0↑/8↓; EPS 1↑/5↓ last month), confirming this is a genuine downgrade cycle not a transient overshoot. [13]
- LT EPS growth is now marked at 2% (peer-median 10%) and one broker cut LT growth from +4.08% to -11.22%, so the low multiple may be EARNED by a genuinely decelerating forward profile rather than a residual mispricing. [55]
- A CEO transition compounds the customer shock — an interim CEO (Jonathan Keyser) was appointed June 1, 2026, one of three discrete gap-down events, signalling leadership instability at the moment of maximum operational stress. [57]
- The trailing EV/EBITDA of 4.7x sits at the 0th percentile of its own ~5y range (10.4–23.2x, median 13.6x) — but this trailing multiple is fully consistent with a permanent forward step-down: FY27E EBITDA is guided/consensus DOWN to $333M from FY26E $382M, so the 'cheap trailing' read is partly the MISREAD and the falling forward is the real signal. [17]

### Decisive question
**Does the Avis termination represent a one-time step-down after which the remaining ~$850M franchise re-stabilizes and grows off a self-funding base, or is it the first crack in a decelerating, single-customer-exposed revenue profile that keeps the multiple permanently depressed?**

### …evidence for
- Management already reset FY26 guidance to include the loss and preserved positive FCF ($140–150M) and adj-EPS ($1.19–1.25), consistent with a business that re-bases rather than spirals. [10]
- The two other core segments continued to build EBITDA into FY25 (Commercial Services $268M→$283M; Government Solutions steady ~$122M), showing the non-Avis base was still growing pre-shock. [58]

### …evidence against
- FY27E revenue and EBITDA are guided/consensus LOWER than FY26E ($948M and $333M vs $989M and $382M), implying the market expects the decline to continue past the one-time Avis reset. [59]
- Concentration risk persists: NYC DOT is still 15.2% of revenue per the latest 10-Q, and multiple named ground-transport customers (Avis, Hertz, Enterprise) share the same cyclical exposure that just cost the Avis contract. [7]

### Key unknowns
- Whether the ~$4.7x trailing / ~4.3x forward EV/EBITDA at a 0th-percentile own-history floor is overshoot or earned remains unresolved — a trailing multiple at its floor is neither bull nor bear until the forward base stabilizes.
- The peer set is HETEROGENEOUS (caps $430M–$15.9B, 37x span) so every peer-median multiple is a confidence-gated input, not a clean benchmark; the earned-vs-residual split cannot be cleanly read off this comp.
- Covenant basis and covenant headroom are UNKNOWN/not disclosed — an expected disclosure gap that is a bearish risk given BB- rating and 3.0x total-debt/EBITDA if the forward EBITDA base erodes further.
